The reranking stage, tested the JudgeSaab way. A weak first-stage retriever proposes candidate authorities; the reranker reorders them. What matters is whether the controlling authority — the provision the court's holding actually turns on — ends up at the top. The headline metric is controlling@1. The ground truth is intrinsic to the case record: a case's cited authorities are its human.laws, and the first-listed is the controlling one — no hand-labeling. Runs entirely on your device, instantly. Nothing is mocked: real BM25 / TF-IDF / RRF / MMR over the real authority corpus.

The first stage is deliberately weak (token overlap), so the controlling authority is usually in the candidate pool but not at the top — the reranker's job is to fix that. Deterministic and instant: results update as you change any control.

How it works

  1. First stage — a weak retriever (token overlap) proposes the top-N candidate authorities.
  2. Rerank — the reranker reorders those candidates using stronger signals (BM25, rank fusion, MMR).
  3. Ground truth — the controlling authority (first-listed in the case record) is the target; other cited authorities get partial credit.
  4. Score — controlling@1, controlling@k, controlling MRR, graded nDCG@k, recall@k.
  5. Compare — see which reranker most reliably lifts the controlling precedent to the top.

API on window.JudgeSaabReranking: runReranking(), compareRerankers(). Part of the JudgeSaab pipeline-test family — one legal corpus, a ground-truth-anchored test per stage (compression, retrieval, reranking, …). Real algorithms over the real corpus; nothing is mocked.
